Yong et al. (2023) - Nanosecond repetitively pulsed discharge, CO2 batch reactor, 3.5-12 bar
Source ID: SRC023. Plasma Sources Sci. Technol. 32, 115012. Published version (IOP).
Experimental + 0D kinetic model (model not used as evidence). Pure CO2. Stanford / EM2C.

Scale: smallest energy input in the set: 300-1000 uJ pulses at 30 kHz
(~9-30 W deposited) into a 200 (or 400) cm3 batch chamber; ~48 mmol CO2 per
420 s batch (~0.007 mol/min equivalent). Power = energy deposited in the gap
(V and I probes); pulser efficiency not reported. Not a flow reactor.

Conversion: up to 14% at 23% efficiency (5.8 bar, 700 uJ, 420 s);
saturates at ~18% over long times; falls with pressure at fixed energy
(8% -> 2% from 3.5 to 12 bar). GC with N_CO ~ 2 N_O2, no gas by-products;
carbon balance assumed (electrode deposits not quantified).

Energy (deposited energy):
- eta ~20% for conversion <15%, insensitive to pressure, pulse energy and
  time; ~2x earlier NRP work. EC_CO ~15-16 kWh/Nm3 at the best point.
- Volume question: the headline and 9 bar data recalculate with 200 cm3;
  the 5 bar time series (incl. the 27% point) only with 400 cm3; the text's
  best-efficiency inputs (Phi 4%, 865 uJ, 420 s) give 4-8%. Human check.

Durability: electrode gap grew 150 -> 250 um in 240 s with carbon deposits.

Reporting quality: conversion/product results moderate confidence (calibrated
GC, N_CO ~ 2 N_O2; carbon balance assumed); energy results lower
confidence (deposited energy only; volume per data set unclear).

Traceability: manuscript.pdf pp. 2-16, Figs. 3, 8, 11, 13; SRC023-E001-E015.
